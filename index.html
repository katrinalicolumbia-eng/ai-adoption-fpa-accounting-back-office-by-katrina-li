The method

Don’t ask employees to write context. Earn it through one workflow that unlocks the next, and save it as skills as you go.

Back-office work is reconciliation: every system has to tell the same story, and every number gets checked again. The logic for that lives in people’s heads. Nobody will write it down, but they will grant access when a task needs it and answer a yes/no question in five seconds.

Old way: ask staff to write skills or a full guide

  • It takes hours on top of the month-end close.
  • It goes stale within a quarter.
  • It makes the writer replaceable.

So it never gets written.

New way: grant access, answer yes or no

ClaudeYou’ve cut Q4 revenue by 20% in each of the past 3 years. Is that because enterprise implementations slip into Q1? Do it again?
FP&A leadYes, only for enterprise contracts signed after September.
ClaudeSaved as a rule (owner: you). To check implementation dates I need read-only CRM access. OK?
Rule saved · owner: FP&A leadAccess asked: CRM, read-onlyStaff time: 5 seconds

Why management accounting is the right way in

1 · Their goal is operational insight

  • Travel +18% on flat headcount → tighten the travel policy
  • Marketing +$5m from 3 events that brought no new leads → cut them
  • Cost per loan +12% in one unit → reprice or restaff

2 · Natural motivation, no fear

  • They are judged on the conclusions, and they sign off.
  • Claude does the 80-report grind; they present sharper insights, faster.
  • It makes them look smarter. Accounting staff, by contrast, fear replacement.

3 · Their work pulls in everyone’s data

  • To explain any driver, Claude needs accounting’s ledger, sub-ledgers and close files, and treasury’s cash data.
  • Example: the travel spike → read access to the expense sub-ledger → Claude learns how accounting codes and accrues travel.
Result: one team’s monthly report becomes the door into accounting and treasury workflows, without anyone writing documentation.

Where to start

Start where the gap is biggest and where a way in already exists.

Second-tier banks hire Goldman-trained people and want Goldman-grade control, but they can’t build Goldman’s systems. So the work is still manual, and that is the gap.
